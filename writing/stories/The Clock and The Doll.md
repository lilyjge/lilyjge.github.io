The Clock and The Doll  
	In a shop stuffed with relics of the past, a little girl wandered around aimlessly. An ancient perfume haunted the air, the kind one suffocates from in a grandmother’s parlour, making her sneeze. The dim yellow lighting shrouded the antiques with the promise of age and threatened to capture her vibrant youth, too. Her eyelids fluttered and she yawned as the classical music droned on. The mountains of musty effects that could occupy her mother for hours cast gloomy shadows on the walls as she passed by without hesitation.   
Why wasn’t there anything *fun*? Anything she could play with? The girl sighed and tried to recall how many times she had walked in circles already, as she once again approached the register where her mother was bargaining with the shopkeeper.   
	“I apologize, madam, but I truly cannot go any lower,” the elderly man said. “It would undermine the value of its history and fabled properties.”  
	“The history, I respect,” said the girl’s mother. “But I won't be charged for superstitions\!”  
	The item in question was a clock ticking away on the counter, as big as the girl’s head and almost as lovely as its childish sweetness, but in elegance. Elaborate golden patterns ran over the smooth reddish brown case, framing an ornate face with roman numerals and intricate hands that went round and round. It seemed older and newer than all other objects in the store at the same time. For one entranced moment, the girl could not move her gaze.   
	*Tick-tock.*   
	*Tick-tock.*   
	*Tick-tock.*  
	Yet it was only a clock, and a second later the girl had roamed out of the old shop for the cheerful mall, taking advantage of her mother busily arguing with the shopkeeper. The suddenly bright lights made her blink but were not unwelcome. She took a deep breath of the relatively fresh air and giggled. Skipping to the upbeat music and her newfound freedom, she explored to her heart’s content before stopping short in front of a display window.  
	“So pretty\!” she exclaimed.  
	A doll with large blue eyes, platinum blonde curls, a petite hat, and a frilly pink dress with cute bows and shiny gems all over stood behind the glass. The girl pressed her face against the cool pane. She did not need to be good at math to know that the big number on the tag was too high for her mother to agree to buy, so she would try to look at it for as long as possible.   
	“Do you want that?”  
	The girl turned, eyes widening and backing up a few steps. Her first impression of the speaker was that she looked familiar, though she couldn’t remember how. She was tall and thin and looked to be in her early twenties, wearing strange clothes and a gentle smile, with burn marks and scars completely covering one arm. Her hands fidgeted, her brows twitched, and her eyes darted about. The girl bit her lip, recalling her mother’s warning about strangers, but glanced back towards the doll. She nodded emphatically.  
	“Wait here,” the nice lady said.   
	She went into the store and returned with the doll, handing it to the girl, who narrowed her eyes at first, but then slowly reached out and took the toy. Its dress was soft and flouncy, its plastic skin hard and cold, and its weight comfortably heavy. The girl hugged it tightly to her chest and grinned from ear to ear.  
	“T-thank you\!” she said shyly.   
	“You’re very welcome,” said the young woman. “I had one just like it, when I was your age. Now, why don’t we get you back to your mom? She must be worried. And when you see her, could you do me a favor?”  
	“Yes\!”  
	The woman told her what to do, the girl nodded, and the woman smiled again. She took the girl by the hand and led her back to the antique shop, though she never told her that was where she came from. The girl gripped her doll and did not ask questions, fearing that the woman would change her mind and take back the gift.   
Before the entrance of the store, the woman let go of her hand. Her mother had finally finished bargaining with a satisfied beam and was paying. The girl peeked up and her brows lifted. Tears were trailing silently down the woman’s ghostly pale cheeks, and the look in her eyes was enough to make the girl shiver. The girl tilted her head and opened her lips, but the woman smiled through her tears and pushed her forward lightly.   
“Don’t worry about me,” she said. “Go on, your mom’s right there.”  
The girl obeyed and ran to hug her mother, who picked her up and kissed her forehead. From her glow, she had successfully won the battle, and the shopkeeper was wrapping up the clock with a sigh.   
“I’m not feeling well, Mommy,” the girl said. “Can we go home?”  
“Of course, honey,” her mother stroked her hair. “You should’ve told me earlier. Oh, where did you get this doll?”  
	“Nice lady gave me.”  
The girl turned her head, but the woman was gone.   
Later that night, the girl was watching television with her mother, curled up next to her and clutching her new doll. The news was on.  
“Headline of the day,” said the anchor. “Explosion in the parking lot of the local mall this afternoon. Fortunately, no one was injured…”  
Pictures flashed on the screen with more details of the incident. The girl snuggled closer to her mother, who wrapped an arm around her.  
“That’s where we were parked\!” her mother cried. “And we left just before it, too. What luck\!”  
The new clock sat on the mantel under the TV, marching on. The girl caught a glimpse of her reflection in its glass and seemed to see the woman smiling back.
